method

how a number gets onto this page.

  1. one test produces every line: swift test -c release, the golden test. it needs the model prepared and the golden tensors. there are no other tests.
  2. the reference is hugging face transformers at one pinned commit, running the pinned checkpoint on the cpu in 32-bit float with a batch of one. it is frozen in golden/.
  3. a run passes only if all three clips give the reference's token ids and text, stop at the same token, give bit-identical scores on repeat, alone, in a wide batch, after a slot refill and after a reload, and keep every beam comparison inside its safety margin. a run that fails any of these is logged as failed and never counts as a result.
  4. each clip runs five times. the first is the cold run. the median of the four warm runs is the wall time, and speed is the clip's audio length divided by it. time in mel, encode, prefill and decode is the best warm run.
  5. release builds, a quiet machine and a nominal thermal state. the thermal state is logged with every run. this mac has no fan, so long runs can slow down as it heats up, which is why sustained throughput is recorded apart from the single-clip speeds.
  6. a tie is within 3%. only a change that is exact and beats noise is kept.
  7. timing runs go one at a time. the machine's state is in each run's label when it was not quiet.

the log is docs/performance.jsonl, one json line per run. this page is rebuilt from it with swift scripts/performance.swift.

where it stands

ceiling, decodeeach step reads 3.44 gb of weights (2.82 gb of layers and 0.62 gb for the output head) plus the cache: 229 kb per context token per beam, about 1.2 gb for three beams at a 1,700-token context. at the measured 79 to 91 gb/s that caps a single clip near 26 steps per second with a short context and near 20 with a long one.
now, decode19.2 to 21.7 steps per second, about 85 to 95 percent of that ceiling. three experiments in a row landed inside noise, so this is a plateau.
ceiling, prefill and encoderbound by gpu arithmetic. apple's matrix kernel measured 2.7 tflops here. a 2-minute prefill is about 5.9 tflop of work, so about 2.2 s at best, against 2.3 s now. a short prefill is about 0.24 s, of which 88 ms is widening the weights to 32-bit.
what limits a wide batchpast about 12 rows the step is bound by gpu arithmetic, not memory. sharing weight reads is worth about 1.3x to 1.4x in total throughput on this mac, not the 12 to 20x the plan guessed.
what the plan's goals gotsame accuracy: met on the three golden clips, with at most 36 percent of the safety margin used. speed: 4.3x to 6.3x realtime, at the top of the plan's 4 to 6x estimate. throughput: 6.2x to 6.3x. latency: 0.30 s to first text on a short file. live first draft 0.7 to 1.1 s against a goal of about 0.5 s, not met.
next lever 1a cheaper live draft pass. a pass costs about 60 ms to encode, 236 ms to prefill and 45 ms per new token. reuse between passes only helps phrases longer than one 8-second encoder window, so the first draft needs a different idea, such as keeping a 32-bit copy of the decoder weights (5.6 gb) to skip the 88 ms widening.
next lever 2log-softmax and top-k on the gpu, to drop the 1.8 mb logits readback per step. small.
not measuredenergy, an instruments trace of where work ran, the noise run through a real input, and behaviour once the fanless mac is hot for minutes. every run so far is in thermal state fair.
not verifiedthe real microphone flow and its permission prompt, voice processing, a full first-run download of the 4 gb checkpoint, and voiceover in the app.
