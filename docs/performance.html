method

how a number gets onto this page.

  1. one test produces every line: swift test -c release, the golden test. it needs the model prepared and the golden tensors. there are no other tests.
  2. the reference is hugging face transformers at one pinned commit, running the pinned checkpoint on the cpu in 32-bit float with a batch of one. it is frozen in golden/.
  3. a run passes only if all three clips give the reference's token ids and text, stop at the same token, give bit-identical scores on repeat, alone, in a wide batch, after a slot refill and after a reload, and keep every beam comparison inside its safety margin. a run that fails any of these is logged as failed and never counts as a result.
  4. each clip runs five times. the first is the cold run. the median of the four warm runs is the wall time, and speed is the clip's audio length divided by it. time in mel, encode, prefill and decode is the best warm run.
  5. release builds, a quiet machine and a nominal thermal state. the thermal state is logged with every run. this mac has no fan, so long runs can slow down as it heats up, which is why sustained throughput is recorded apart from the single-clip speeds.
  6. a tie is within 3%. only a change that is exact and beats noise is kept.
  7. timing runs go one at a time. the machine's state is in each run's label when it was not quiet.

the log is docs/performance.jsonl, one json line per run. this page is rebuilt from it with swift scripts/performance.swift.

where it stands

ceiling, decodeeach step reads 3.44 gb of weights (2.82 gb of layers and 0.62 gb for the output head) plus the cache: 229 kb per context token per beam, about 1.2 gb for three beams at a 1,700-token context. at the measured 79 to 91 gb/s that caps a single clip near 26 steps per second with a short context and near 20 with a long one.
now, decode19.9 to 21.8 steps per second for one clip, about 85 to 95 percent of that ceiling. this is a plateau.
ceiling, prefill and encoderbound by gpu arithmetic. apple's matrix kernel measured 2.7 tflops here. a 2-minute prefill is about 5.9 tflop of work, so about 2.2 s at best, against 2.3 s now. a short prefill is about 0.24 s, of which 88 ms is widening the weights to 32-bit.
what limits a wide batchwith three rows per thread sharing each weight read, a 12-row step costs about what a 3-row step did before: 51 ms against 103 ms. twelve medium clips run at 12.4x realtime cool and 9.8x warm, against 6x one at a time. wider than 12 rows gains nothing, and long clips gain less, because each new piece is still encoded and read in alone between steps.
what the plan's goals gotsame accuracy: met on the three golden clips, with at most 34 percent of the safety margin used. speed: 4.3x to 6.7x realtime for one clip. throughput: 12.4x for many short files cool, inside the plan's 12 to 20x estimate, 7.2x for the three golden clips together, and 11.3x for them three times over. latency: 0.30 s to first text on a short file. live first text 0.48 to 0.79 s, then token by token.
next lever 1a scheduler that refills in groups. reading several pieces in with one prefill is built and bit-identical, and takes 27 to 39 percent less gpu time for groups of short pieces, but twelve clips at once gained nothing with it, so the scheduler still reads each new piece in alone. it pays only if pieces are held back to arrive together.
next lever 2log-softmax and top-k on the gpu, to drop the 1.8 mb logits readback per step. small.
next lever 3the wait between live draft passes. each pass re-encodes the phrase, prefills it and re-decodes the unsettled tail before new text appears: 0.5 to 0.9 s at the 90th percentile. the 88 ms of weight widening per prefill is the cheapest part to remove, at the cost of 5.6 gb for a 32-bit copy.
not measuredenergy, an instruments trace of where work ran, the noise run through a real input, and a proper cool-against-hot comparison. forty minutes of back-to-back runs took sustained throughput from about 6x to 4.3x on this fanless mac.
not verifiedon a real iphone: on an iphone 17 pro the three golden clips give the same token ids and candidate scores as the mac and the reference, measured with a benchmark build. the golden test itself has not run there, and the microphone, listen and record are untried. also unverified anywhere: voice processing, a full first-run download of the 4 gb checkpoint, and voiceover.
